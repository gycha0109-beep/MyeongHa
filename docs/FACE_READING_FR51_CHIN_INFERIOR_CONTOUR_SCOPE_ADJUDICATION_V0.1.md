# FR-51 — Chin inferior contour anatomical scope adjudication

## Status

FR-51 resolves the scope-class ambiguity left by FR-50.

Authority state:

`central_inferior_soft_tissue_chin_scope_class_selected_exact_endpoints_dense_curve_and_provider_binding_blocked`

Selected FR-35 anatomical scope class:

`central_inferior_soft_tissue_chin_boundary`

This is narrower than a full lower jawline running from the ear region to Menton. The decision is based on the traditional consumer requirement for `地閣` plus modern neutral facial morphology. It does not equate a traditional label with a modern curve.

## 1. Upstream state

FR-49 established:

- traditional `地閣` belongs to the chin/頦 region;
- lower 三停 can terminate at 地閣 in the pinned transmitted witness;
- soft-tissue Menton is defined from the soft-tissue contour of the chin;
- traditional 地閣 does not equal soft-tissue Menton;
- a Menton point cannot substitute for a curve.

FR-50 established:

- provider-independent lower-jaw/chin contour operationalizations exist;
- a broad lower jawline can be represented using curve semilandmarks;
- bilateral Menton-side plus Menton can provide a sparse central-inferior chin scaffold;
- those operationalization families must remain distinct;
- the exact FR-35 anatomical scope was still unresolved.

FR-51 therefore asks:

> Which anatomical extent best matches the traditional consumer requirement behind FR-35: a broad lower jawline or the central inferior soft-tissue chin boundary?

## 2. Traditional transmitted witness

Primary transmitted compilation used for this adjudication:

- `欽定古今圖書集成`
- `博物彙編 藝術典 第六百三十六卷`
- `相術部彙考六`
- `神相全編六〈神異賦〉`
- Wikisource scan transcription, Volume 473.

Important source boundary:

This is a transmitted compilation witness. FR-51 does not treat it as proof of original authorship, original dating, or the earliest surviving wording of `神異賦`.

### Scan page 48

Source:

`https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Gujin_Tushu_Jicheng,_Volume_473_(1700-1725).djvu/48`

Relevant transmitted statement:

`頦為地閣`

Safe reading:

- traditional 地閣 is centered on 頦/chin terminology;
- the passage does not define a modern point or curve;
- it does not describe the full ear-to-chin lower jawline as 地閣.

### Scan page 49

Source:

`https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Gujin_Tushu_Jicheng,_Volume_473_(1700-1725).djvu/49`

Relevant transmitted relations include:

- `地閣在承漿之下，頤頦之間`
- `地閣為頦`
- separate discussion of `重頤豐頷` and broad bilateral lower-face fullness.

Safe reading:

- 地閣 is located in the lower central chin context below 承漿;
- 頤/頷 and broader bilateral lower-face fullness remain separately expressible concepts;
- the text does not provide metric lateral endpoints.

FR-51 intentionally does not map 承漿, 頤, 頦, or 地閣 one-to-one to modern anthropometric landmarks.

### Scan page 54

Source:

`https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Gujin_Tushu_Jicheng,_Volume_473_(1700-1725).djvu/54`

Relevant transmitted statement:

`地庫在兩頤`

This is important because the same traditional text uses the bilateral `兩頤` under a distinct named area, `地庫`. Therefore, treating every lateral lower-jaw/cheek surface as 地閣 would erase a distinction actually preserved by the witness.

## 3. Historical anatomical corroboration

Source:

- `御纂醫宗金鑒`
- `正骨心法要旨`
- `地閣骨`
- `https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?chapter=882017&if=en`

The medical text identifies `地閣骨` with `頦` and the colloquial `下巴骨`.

Safe admission:

- independent historical terminology again places 地閣/頦 in the chin/lower-anterior mandibular area.

Not admitted:

- historical bony terminology does not prove that traditional soft-tissue 地閣 equals modern Menton;
- it does not define the FR-35 soft-tissue curve.

## 4. Modern neutral morphology — central chin scaffold

Source:

- Zupan et al. 2022
- `An evaluation of three-dimensional facial changes after surgically assisted rapid maxillary expansion (SARME): an observational study`
- DOI `10.1186/s12903-022-02179-1`
- PMCID `PMC9063160`

The 3D facial landmark protocol separately defines:

- `Menton`: most inferior midpoint of the chin;
- left/right `Menton side`: where the vertical through the respective Cheilion reaches the lowest point of the chin;
- left/right `Gonion`: a different, more lateral mandibular-angle landmark.

This gives FR-51 a provider-independent modern distinction between:

1. a central inferior chin scaffold; and
2. a broader lateral mandibular-angle geometry.

The fact that Menton-side and Gonion are separately defined is useful for scope adjudication, but Menton-side is not promoted as a traditional 地閣 boundary.

## 5. Modern neutral morphology — broader jawline contrast

Source:

- Windhager et al. 2019
- `Facial aging trajectories: A common shape pattern in male and female faces is disrupted after menopause`
- DOI `10.1002/ajpa.23878`
- PMCID `PMC6771603`

The study defines each lower jawline using sliding semilandmarks between Otobasion inferius and Menton along the lower border of the mandible.

This is deliberately retained as a contrast class:

`broader_lower_jawline`

It is a valid provider-independent geometric construction, but it is materially broader than the central chin region supported by the traditional consumer evidence. FR-51 therefore rejects it as the FR-35 consumer scope while preserving it as a separate neutral morphology construct.

## 6. FR-51 scope decision

Selected:

`central_inferior_soft_tissue_chin_boundary`

Required scope property:

- includes soft-tissue Menton as the inferior-midline neutral anchor.

Excluded as required FR-35 extent/endpoints:

- mandibular-angle Gonion;
- ear-region Otobasion inferius;
- the entire Otobasion-inferius-to-Menton lower jawline.

Research-compatible but not authoritative endpoints:

- left/right Menton-side from FR-50.

This means the FR-50 three-point scaffold:

`left_menton_side -> soft_tissue_menton -> right_menton_side`

is now classified as:

`compatible_with_selected_scope_class_not_exact_lateral_endpoints_or_dense_curve`

## 7. What FR-51 resolves

FR-51 now authorizes these narrow claims:

1. the FR-35 anatomical scope class is no longer ambiguous between central chin and full lower jawline;
2. the selected class is the central inferior soft-tissue chin boundary;
3. soft-tissue Menton is required as its inferior-midline neutral anchor;
4. the broad Otobasion-inferius-to-Menton lower jawline is not the FR-35 consumer scope;
5. Gonion is not a required FR-35 endpoint;
6. the FR-50 Menton-side/Menton scaffold is compatible research geometry for this selected scope class.

## 8. What FR-51 does not resolve

FR-51 leaves all of the following blocked:

- exact left/right FR-35 endpoints;
- Menton-side as the final endpoint rule;
- dense or continuous contour geometry;
- sampling density;
- interpolation;
- smoothing;
- canonical image-normalized 2D extraction;
- empirical contour-error tolerance;
- minimum validation subject count;
- MediaPipe FACE_OVAL subset selection;
- MediaPipe index `152 == Menton`;
- traditional `地閣 == selected neutral curve`;
- traditional `地閣 == soft-tissue Menton`;
- production 三停 / F1 / F6 / geometry.

The following protocol fields therefore remain `null`:

- `endpointSelectionRule`
- `interpolationMethod`
- `smoothingMethod`
- `providerSubsetRule`
- `minimumSubjectCount`
- `maximumAllowedContourError`

## 10. Next evidence

The next blocker is now sharply defined:

> What provider-independent rule defines the exact left and right endpoints of the selected central inferior soft-tissue chin boundary in an image-space reference curve?

A valid next slice must not guess those endpoints from MediaPipe. It should first search neutral anatomy / facial anthropometry for a reproducible central-chin boundary definition and only then create provider-blind reference traces or endpoint annotations.

After that neutral reference curve is frozen, a provider subset may be scored against it as a separate validation problem.
